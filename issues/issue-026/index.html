Objections raised, and what happened to each

Three objections were raised against the human freeze. Two landed as floor and were accepted into landing v1.1. One did not land as floor.

  1. First-author bury (“Buehler and colleagues”). LANDS. Accepted. The landing now names Subhadeep Pal, Fiona Y. Wang, and Markus J. Buehler.
  2. Isolated baseline misstated as a compute-matched singleton versus the paper’s parallel one-agent search. LANDS. Accepted. The landing now states: the paper’s isolated baseline was parallel one-agent search on the same decision schedule, not one agent given the society’s total compute; isolated search could still keep the strongest single artifact; a compute-matched singleton was not tested.
  3. “Collective brains” uncited. Did not land as floor. Left as the author wrote it.

Floor and what matched

No floor block on the human essay. The 95% stigmergic first reuse, the agent-free disturbance assay, and the portfolio versus champion-artifact bound all match the paper.

The two accepted floor rows are the whole of the human redline. The landing is post-audit clean text. This page is where the freeze is visible, so the author does not get credit for audit fixes as original wording.

What this audit did: check the human essay against the preprint and apply two floor corrections on a published redline. What it did not do: treat the machine inverse as a second edition, rewrite “collective brains,” or designate the issue high-stakes in the Issue 25 sense.
