Objections raised, and what happened to each

Five objections. All landed. All accepted into landing v1.1.

MAJOR “hundreds” versus N=50 / 100 / 200. LANDS

The freeze said the experiment put hundreds of initially identical frontier agents into the world. The paper reports populations of 50, 100, and 200.

Accepted. Landing now names those three population sizes.

MAJOR “past twelve forks” versus a 12-edge deepest representative path. LANDS

The freeze overstated the genealogy as running past twelve forks. The paper’s figure is a deepest representative genealogy of 12 fork edges.

Accepted. Landing now states that figure exactly.

KILL Fused assays; knockout read as live function; “caretaker” as a gloss. LANDS

The freeze treated the held-out functional assay and the 98.3% / 59.6% topological knockout as one event, implied remaining connectedness meant remaining function, and called the surviving node a caretaker. Knockout is graph connectivity, not live function.

Accepted by splitting the two tests and dropping the function claim. Floor FAIL until that split; the split is now in landing v1.1.

MAJOR Omitted isolated-search champion-artifact bound. LANDS

The freeze reported the society winning on portfolio breadth and left out the paper’s bound: isolated search could still keep the strongest single artifact.

Accepted. Landing now states the bound in place.

MAJOR Attribution symmetry. LANDS

Not in the original machine-audit list. The author later required Pal, Wang, and Buehler on both landings. The freeze named “Markus Buehler, writing with graduate students at MIT.”

Accepted. Landing names Subhadeep Pal, Fiona Y. Wang, and Markus J. Buehler. Follow-ons: “Buehler’s setup / measured edges” become “The setup / The measured edges.”
