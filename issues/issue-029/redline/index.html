R1 · Lede twin author polish

Authorized by the author, 2026-09-20 PT. Thesis and early twin kept in lockstep with leftover accompaniment, not “what it feels like” as a mystery-object.

The answer to the hard problem of consciousness may be the one we least want to hear: the mystery of subjective experience the leftover — experience treated as an extra accompaniment to the brain’s jobs — isn’t a puzzle missing a metaphysical piece, but a flawed premise masquerading as a question.

In other words: the famous mystery of “what it feels like to be you” leftover — why the brain’s jobs should be accompanied by experience at all, rather than run in the dark — may not be a missing piece of the world. It may be a bad setup wearing a deep-question costume.

R2 · Chalmers residual author polish

Authorized by the author, 2026-09-20 PT. Residual retargeted from leftover-feel as mystery-object to why-accompanied / why not in the dark.

…and something is still supposed to be left over. The red of red. The hurt of a scraped knee. Not another job. The accompaniment. Why the work should not run in the dark. Why vivid red rather than the same performance with the lights off. Some people add a soul to cover the leftover.

The hard problem treats the hidden bug as the whole point. It asks: how does a brain make the bug? The hard problem treats that accompaniment as the hidden bug. It asks: why do the jobs come with the bug at all? Why not in the dark?

R3 · Third-slot “behind” author polish

Authorized by the author, 2026-09-20 PT. Dropped the third-slot “behind all of that” wording.

The leftover label that was supposed to sit behind all of that, and beat every plain description, The leftover that was supposed to accompany the jobs — experience as an extra the functions were said to leave out — is the part that never earned its keep.

R4 · Preferred close author polish

Authorized by the author, 2026-09-20 PT. Close locked verbatim.

The mystery of what it feels like to be you is not a missing piece of the world. The missing piece was never missing. It is a flawed premise masquerading as a question.
