Quote and claim notes

Audit-page notes only. Ruling A (2026-10-04): these notes do not change the freeze. Notes 4 and 5 are on the machine companion’s audit.

  1. Author quote. “Super-permeated” is Saunders’s word for his taste. The freeze gives it to his sensibility. In the interview, sensibility is what the choices imprint: “It’s the thousands of micro-choices I’ve made over the weeks or months or years of working like this that make the story ‘mine’—that imprint my sensibility into the thing. My taste has super-permeated the thing.”
  2. Author quote. The interview prints Bartleby as “I [would] prefer not to.” The freeze drops the brackets.
  3. Author quote, minor. “Thousands of micro-choices” is not the interview’s span (“the thousands of micro-choices I’ve made”).
  4. Author fact, soft. “About a quarter” of eighth- and twelfth-graders at Proficient matches NAEP Writing 2011 (24 percent). “Most recent” is true for an assessment of both grades: the 2017 writing assessment covered grades 4 and 8 only, not grade 12. The year is not in the essay. At or above Proficient, including Advanced, is 27 percent at both grades. The essay does not claim that NAEP Proficient means on-grade-level proficiency.
  5. Author fact, soft. “Spending less cognitive effort” matches the headline of Lee et al., CHI 2025, and flattens a conditional, self-reported result. The survey is of 319 knowledge workers. The reduction is stronger when confidence in the AI is high; on some high-stakes tasks, workers report more effort than doing the task without AI. The essay does not name CHI. The next sentence already treats better writing and less thinking as able to happen together.

  1. The title promises more disagreement than the close delivers. Both readers. The title says Saunders may be wrong about the rest of us. The close says the choice is murkier and that refusal is not better for everyone. LANDS as audit-only. Ruling A. Argument. Not a reason to rewrite.
  2. The “muddle or silence” sentence leaves out templates, editors, and imperfect prose. Both readers. LANDS as audit-only. Argument.
  3. The six-month anecdote carries more of the on-ramp claim than the studies do. Both readers. The anecdote is offered as one case and then cut down. The studies are real; the floor pass already checked them. The objection is about how hard the essay leans, not about an invented paper. LANDS as audit-only. Argument.

The synthesis records no further single-leg objections, and it records no false citation and no internal contradiction that reverses a sentence. Quote and claim notes above were already in the floor pass. Stage C did not turn them into freeze edits.

Floor and what matched

Floor fails on the author essay: 0. Invented citations: 0. Unverified named studies: 0. The legal first name does not appear. Length is inside the 800–1,200 band (1,057 words). There is no accepted floor row and therefore no wording on the human redline. The landing is the freeze.

Interview spans that match verbatim: “imagine me banging my cane on the floor”; “not for a second”; “even in an email”; “By my definition of writing, writing with AI isn’t. Isn’t writing.”; “blurry crap”; “precisely what I mean, and then some”; “human-to-human, highly advanced, nuanced code”; “language is power”; “by taste”; “force-fed Muzak all the time, even in concert halls”; “the aesthetic adjusts downward”; “dull and literal.” “Asked readers” is a fair gloss of the published opening. “Is how a person discovers what they believe” joins the transformative-revision sentence to a separate sentence about finding out what one believes. That join is paraphrase, not a fake quote. The voice-imitation use is argument, and it is consistent with the “not for a second” answer, which was about writing or revising with AI. He had, separately, asked a model to imitate his voice. The essay treats that later.

Kosmyna et al. 2025: 54 participants in sessions 1–3 (18 in session 4), three arms (LLM, search, brain-only). The lab’s wording is connectivity and cognitive activity. “Weakest brain engagement” is a common gloss, and “small” and “preliminary” match the preprint. The essay does not invent the 83 percent figure that appears in some secondary write-ups. Mahapatra 2024: ChatGPT as formative feedback had a significant positive impact on undergraduate ESL students’ academic writing skills. The 33-student abstract matches grammar, vocabulary, content organization, reduced anxiety, and greater confidence. “Found improvements” follows the abstract’s “enhances.” The design described there is mixed-methods perception, and “organization” shortens “content organization.” The essay gives no year, so a 2026 index date is not a conflict. “Six months after I started writing with AI, I began writing pieces entirely on my own” is first-person testimony, offered as one case and then cut down. “Models’ tendency to flatter and agree” names no paper, so it is not an invented citation.
