# Stage C — machine-leg-b-claude-opus-4.1

## Objections

### 1. Confirmation caveat undermines entire premise
**Quote:** "I can't independently confirm the flight, so I am taking it as described and assuming the upper stage returned from orbital speed and was caught by its launch tower."

**Objection:** The essay builds an elaborate ranking and future projection on an event the author admits they cannot verify happened as described. This isn't a minor detail—whether the upper stage actually returned from orbital speed fundamentally changes the achievement being ranked.

**Why it lands:** A reader would reasonably question why we should accept any subsequent analysis when the foundational event might not have occurred as claimed.

**Severity:** FLOOR

### 2. "Modern tools" rule appears arbitrary and inconsistently applied
**Quote:** "With 2026 computers, materials and factories, the pyramids and the Panama Canal become large but routine contracts."

**Objection:** The essay never explains why "modern tools" should be the standard for ranking historical engineering achievements, nor how this standard is applied uniformly. Why do chip factories get "no discount" while the Manhattan Project "shrinks"? Both use modern technology.

**Why it lands:** The ranking methodology seems designed to produce a predetermined outcome rather than following clear principles.

**Severity:** ARGUMENT

### 3. Missing justification for specific rankings
**Quote:** "Starship's catch comes sixth, ahead of the International Space Station, which a ship this size would have made far simpler to build."

**Objection:** No clear metrics explain why catching a rocket ranks exactly sixth, above ISS but below LIGO. The ISS comparison assumes Starship's payload capabilities without establishing them.

**Why it lands:** Readers expect concrete reasoning for such specific placements, not just assertions.

**Severity:** ARGUMENT

### 4. Contradictory treatment of reusability achievement
**Quote:** "If this ship flies again within days, I would move it up."

**Objection:** The essay celebrates removing the "main obstacle for sixty years" of single-use vehicles, but then admits this catch hasn't actually demonstrated reusability yet—the very thing being celebrated.

**Why it lands:** The text wants credit for solving reusability while acknowledging it hasn't been proven.

**Severity:** ARGUMENT

### 5. Cost projections lack supporting evidence
**Quote:** "A fully reusable ship that flies often could bring that to a few hundred or less."

**Objection:** Presents order-of-magnitude cost reductions as straightforward outcomes without addressing maintenance, refurbishment, or operational costs that plagued the "reusable" Shuttle.

**Why it lands:** The essay itself mentions Shuttle's costly repairs but doesn't explain why Starship avoids these issues.

**Severity:** ARGUMENT

### 6. Speculative timeline presented without uncertainty bounds
**Paraphrase:** The 10, 25, 50, and 100-year predictions are stated as "plausible" scenarios.

**Objection:** These specific timelines read like science fiction rather than engineering projection, with no error bars, alternative scenarios, or justification for the timeframes chosen.

**Why it lands:** Readers seeking analysis of an engineering achievement get unsupported fortune-telling instead.

**Severity:** SCOPE

### 7. False precision in technical comparisons
**Quote:** "mirrors so smooth that, if one were enlarged to the size of Germany, its largest bump would stand no more than about a millimeter high"

**Objection:** This appears to be garbled—EUV mirrors have surface roughness measured in fractions of nanometers. Scaling to Germany-size would yield bumps far larger than a millimeter.

**Why it lands:** Technical credibility suffers when specific numbers appear wrong.

**Severity:** HYGIENE

### 8. Unexplained Apollo gap reasoning
**Quote:** "Money and politics explain part of that gap, though not all of it."

**Objection:** Implies there's some additional mysterious difficulty beyond funding and political will, but never says what.

**Why it lands:** Vague hand-waving weakens the argument that Apollo remains uniquely difficult.

**Severity:** HYGIENE

**Total objections: 8**
