# Issue 37 machine essay: selection

*Selection note, written before the freeze. Four isolated runs of the same single message. The author delegated the selection (t293u). At the time of writing, nothing had been frozen or published.*

**Selected: Claude Fable 5.2 (max reasoning), "Where the Starship Catch Ranks, and What It Could Change", unchanged.** 1,192 body words. No combination: no splice clearly improved it, so the candidate is byte-identical to the model output.

**Where each model ranked Flight 15.** Fable: sixth (top five: EUV chip fabrication, Apollo, JWST, LHC, LIGO; ISS seventh). Gemini 4 Carbon: seventh (top five: EUV lithography, global internet/cloud, continental power grid, LHC, LIGO; never names sixth). GPT-6 Astra: sixth (top five: leading-edge chip manufacturing, Apollo, LHC, NIF fusion ignition, JWST). Muse Wonder: sixth (top five: Apollo, EUV chipmaking, LHC, LIGO, JWST).

**Why Fable.** It states its ranking test plainly (what stays hard with 2026 tools: physical limits, many coupled parts, few chances to fix a mistake). It gives two concrete reasons Flight 15 sits below the top five: prototypes could be destroyed and retried, and one catch is not yet reuse (with the Shuttle as the counterexample). It also says what would move the catch up. Its future section is the most concrete of the four for ordinary people: direct-to-phone coverage and wildfire/methane sensing at 10 years; an Antarctic-style lunar base, first Mars crews and exoplanet atmospheres at 25; an offshore-rig-style workforce of several thousand at 50, with orbital trips never becoming "bus fare"; and hundreds of thousands off Earth at 100, framed against aviation's first century. Cost anchors (about $50,000/kg for the Shuttle, a few thousand today, a few hundred possible) are checkable. It closes with honest conditions (Concorde, turnaround, debris, low gravity) and stays optimistic without overpromising.

**Why not the others.** Muse Wonder is a close second, with polished prose, sound reasoning and a disciplined 10/25/50/100 arc, but it has fewer concrete figures. GPT-6 Astra is the most careful and accurate, but it reads like a hedged memo; caveats crowd the optimistic view the question asked for, and it uses a stray bold. Gemini 4 Carbon is vivid but invents event detail (Gulf Coast descent, violet plasma), ranks Flight 15 seventh while listing only five achievements, and its future runs past "realistic" (orbital data centres doing much of AI compute by 2051, business-class-priced orbital berths by 2076, millions in Martian cities by 2126).

**Accuracy notes on Fable (no edits made).** (1) It says it "can't independently confirm the flight" and assumes the ship was caught after orbital-speed return. That is transparent and consistent with prior machine essays, and the same caveat appears in GPT-6 Astra. (2) EUV mirror: "if enlarged to the size of Germany, its largest bump would stand no more than about a millimeter". ZEISS's published figure is about 0.1 mm, so the statement holds as an upper bound but understates the precision tenfold. Flag this for the audit rather than editing it. (3) "Nobody has done it since 1972" is hedged with "as far as I know" and remains true as of today. (4) Booster catches since October 2024, 50,000 tin droplets per second, JWST's 344 single-point failures, LIGO's one ten-thousandth of a proton, more than twice Saturn V thrust, and more than 1 billion airline passengers a century after the Wrights are consistent with public figures.
