Correction note

MegaSyn date. The essay opens: “In 2022, a small drug company ran an experiment it later described as a wake-up call.” The experiment was run in 2021 for the Spiez CONVERGENCE conference (online, September 2021). The results were published in 2022 (Urbina et al., “Dual use of artificial-intelligence-powered drug discovery,” Nature Machine Intelligence, 7 March 2022, doi:10.1038/s42256-022-00465-9). The essay’s “2022” refers to the publication year.

The author ruled on this before landing (2026-09-26, 5:00 PM PT): freeze issue-030-author-FROZEN-v1.0 stays as written, and this note carries the correction. Everything else in the anecdote matched the paper: a small drug company, a model built to avoid toxicity, about 40,000 molecules in under six hours, VX among the results, and the authors’ own phrase “a wake-up call.”

A second, smaller wording point was ruled the same day. The essay says the Belmont Report “still governs research on people in America.” Strictly, the binding rule is the Common Rule (45 CFR 46), which requires coverage decisions consistent with the Belmont principles. The author accepted “governs” as a plain-language gloss. The wording is unchanged.

Objections raised, and what happened to each

Floor objections against the author freeze: none that failed. Stage C ran two legs on each essay — moonshotai/kimi-k3 and anthropic/claude-opus-4.1 (stealth/ox-alpha was not used). Each leg saw exactly one freeze and its title: no audit findings, no seed notes, no other essay. The legs converged on six objections to the author essay. Those publish here under ruling A; they do not rewrite the landing. Dispositions summarize the objections; they do not invent Stage C quotations.

  1. The opener shows a human-flipped objective, not a curious machine. The MegaSyn story proves that discovery tools are dual-use when people change the target. The essay itself says “Only the target changed.” Both legs argued that this supports target-setting and access controls better than it supports ranking an AI’s drive to discover below humanity. The strongest shared pushback. LANDS as audit-only. Argument-scope.
  2. “Truth-seeking” slides between two meanings. Honesty and accuracy on one side, unbounded discovery and experimentation on the other; the Musk foil may mean the first. The essay discloses its narrowing (“Let me be precise”), but both readers still felt the slide. LANDS as audit-only. Argument-scope.
  3. “Humanity” is underspecified. Lives, safety, dignity, and freedom can conflict with one another, and the essay does not say whose welfare wins. LANDS as audit-only. Argument and scope.
  4. The ranking is a false fork. Curiosity with constraints is the obvious third option, and research ethics works as external governance rather than an internal value ranking. LANDS as audit-only. Argument and scope.
  5. The surgeon kicker is under-supported. Surgeons are trustworthy partly because of consent, licensing, and liability, and it is not settled whether an AI is more like a surgeon or a researcher. LANDS as audit-only. Argument-scope.
  6. Historical analogies are compressed. The Nazi experiments and Tuskegee as truth-seeking minus humanity, and research ethics as a strict hierarchy rather than a balance. One leg tagged the Nazi line FLOOR; the audit adjudicated it down to argument and tone. The essay scare-quotes “sought truth” and says “in the narrowest sense,” HHS’s own Belmont text frames the Code as a response to experiments on concentration camp prisoners, and the Declaration of Helsinki says research purposes can never take precedence over participants’ rights and interests. No factual error. LANDS as audit-only. Hygiene and scope.

Single-leg objections, recorded and not adjudicated further: the answer to paternalism is definitional (a humanity-valuer is defined to respect freedom); “honesty stays non-negotiable” gives no rule for when a truth is itself the hazard; “real conflicts are rare” sits awkwardly beside “we have to settle this now”; the foundations metaphor is asserted rather than argued. Neither leg raised the MegaSyn date or the Belmont wording; those came from the floor pass and are handled in the correction note.

The dual gap, stated so the two landings are not merged: the author essay answers the question yes, with a definition — humanity (our lives, safety, dignity, and freedom) must outrank an AI’s drive to discover, and the leading danger is the curious machine. The machine companion answers that the question is a trap — human flourishing is the goal, non-deception is a near-inviolable boundary on how it is pursued, and the leading danger is the kind machine that lies to us and to its overseers for our own good. Both keep honesty non-negotiable. Those are still different theses. Stage C pushed each toward the other’s frame, which is an argument for landing them side by side, not for folding one into the other.

Floor and what matched

Floor fails on the author essay: 0. Invented citations: 0. Named facts matched the fetched sources. Urbina et al. 2022: a small drug company’s model, built to avoid toxicity, was turned to reward it and generated forty thousand candidate molecules in under six hours, including VX and other known chemical warfare agents; the paper calls itself a wake-up call (date: see the correction note). Musk, CNBC, 13 July 2023: “maximally curious, maximally truth-seeking,” and the argument that a curious AI would be pro-humanity because humanity is more interesting; both phrases are verbatim. Nuremberg Code, principle 1: “The voluntary consent of the human subject is absolutely essential.” CDC: the Tuskegee study ran 1932–1972 and enrolled 399 men with syphilis; penicillin was the treatment of choice by 1943 and was not offered. Belmont Report: 1979, a response to Tuskegee (“governs” accepted as a gloss, see above). Declaration of Helsinki: research purposes “can never take precedence over the rights and interests of individual research participants.”

Length is inside the 800–1,200 band under every counting method (887–943 words). Reading level is about grade 6. There is no accepted floor row and therefore no wording on the human redline.
