**Recommendation: Claude Fable 5.2, unchanged and not combined.**
- **It answers both halves directly.** "To what extent": it says real but limited, separating problem-like parts of a decision from self-knowledge parts. "What it implies": gather evidence about yourself rather than deduce.
- **It carries the most checkable evidence of the four:** Terman, Grossmann's wise-reasoning study, Damasio's Elliot, Franklin's cod, Stanovich's bias blind spot, Gilbert's surrogation study, and Grossmann and Kross's Solomon's paradox. Each is a real study, characterized fairly on a first check.
- **It gives concrete prescriptions:** small trials, the record over the self-story, surrogate reports, and the friend's-eye view.
- **It has an original point:** "competence itself" as a liability, because success delays corrective feedback.
- **It counters the author's essay well without echoing it.** It never mentions tradition or institutions. The two essays will read as genuinely independent.
- **Risks:** it is the longest (1,209 words) and drier in tone. Terman's outcome rates are hedged. It cost the most ($1.66).

**The others**
- **Muse Wonder (close second):** the most quotable ("You will win the argument and lose the week"). It has strong practical guardrails: precommitment, base rates and reversible experiments. Its evidence is general rather than named.
- **GPT-6.2 Astra:** the most literary. The essay follows one engineer through a promotion, with one real study (Stanovich). It is lovely, but it answers the question by implication more than directly.
- **Gemini 4 Neon:** well organized, with a good "map vs compass" frame. It is more generic, and its vignettes are composite, not sourced.
