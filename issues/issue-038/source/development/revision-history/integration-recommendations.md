## 1. Ranked additions from the other drafts

### 1) The Perkins and Stanovich evidence (Fable). It patches Neon's thinnest spot: the only evidence is Haidt's metaphor.
- **Borrow** (Fable, ¶ "Start with how reasoning works"):
  > "Haidt cites the Harvard researcher David Perkins, who asked people to generate arguments on contested issues and found that IQ strongly predicted how many they produced, but only for their own side. … Keith Stanovich, who has studied this for decades, finds that the tendency to bend evidence toward what we already believe is essentially unrelated to intelligence. Smart people are as biased as everyone else. They are simply more convincing, above all to themselves."
- **Where:** right after Neon's "It just buys you a better lawyer." (¶5).
- **What it adds:** real data behind "better lawyer". It also quietly fixes the overclaim: smart people aren't *more* biased, just better at defending the bias.
- **Cost:** about +65 words. Drop Fable's direct Perkins quote to save about 20 words and remove a quote that needs verifying.
- **Accuracy:** Haidt reports that IQ was the biggest predictor of the number of my-side arguments but did not raise other-side arguments. Stanovich, West and Toplak (2013) find myside bias largely independent of intelligence. "Harvard researcher" is correct for Perkins.
- **Risk:** low. It slows ¶5 slightly, so keep it to two sentences if space is short.

### 2) What IQ adds beyond power and money (Muse). It patches the confound.
- **Borrow** (Muse, two lines):
  > "An ordinary person produces an excuse. A gifted person produces a philosophy."
  > "Wealth adds opportunity. Power adds insulation. Fame ensures that fewer people are willing to say, 'This is stupid, and you know it.'"
- **Where:**
  - Put the first line in Neon's To-be-sure (¶9), right after "mediocre men in corner offices have been doing both since the dawn of commerce."
  - The second line replaces Neon's "all of which create an insulation chamber where sycophants replace truth-tellers and risk starts to feel abstract" (¶5).
- **What it adds:** it answers the obvious objection ("isn't this just power?") by isolating what intelligence uniquely contributes, and it separates the mechanisms crisply.
- **Cost:** about +12 words for the first line; the swap is roughly neutral (about +5).
- **Risk:** low. Both lines are in the same register as Neon.

### 3) Chesterton's fence, in Muse's version. It strengthens the tradition argument.
- **Borrow** (Muse):
  > "Do not tear down a fence until you understand why it was built. The smarter you are, the easier it becomes to invent a reason the fence is unnecessary. That does not mean you have understood it. It may mean only that you are standing on the other side of something you want."
- **Where:** in Neon ¶8, right after "They are the distilled, battle-tested residue of millions of human experiments in what actually sustains trust, meaning, and stability at scale."
- **What it adds:** a named, widely known principle for the author's "they are there and survived for a reason." It ties tradition back to the thesis: cleverness invents reasons. Muse's version beats Fable's one-liner because it does both.
- **Cost:** about +50 words. Use only the first two sentences for about +30.
- **Risk:** low. Neon's pressure test already mentions Chesterton's fence, so it fits.
- **Optional:** Fable's manioc example (Henrich) is the best concrete proof that traditions encode hidden knowledge, but it costs about 110 words. Skip it unless something else is cut.

### 4) The public-reformer vs. private-rationalizer test (Fable). It sharpens the "tradition was wrong about slavery" rebuttal.
- **Borrow** (Fable):
  > "The reformers who overturned bad traditions argued in public, for principles that applied to everyone, and withstood decades of hostile cross-examination. The rationalizer argues in private, for an exemption that applies to himself, starting tonight."
- **Where:** in Neon ¶9, it replaces "When you break with an ancient norm at great personal cost to protect the vulnerable or reveal an uncomfortable truth, you are exercising principle." Keep Neon's next sentence and its closer, "your inner defense attorney is simply billing overtime."
- **What it adds:** a cleaner, more falsifiable test than "great personal cost". It answers the slavery objection directly: abolitionists argued in public for universal rules.
- **Cost:** about +10 net.
- **Risk:** low. It is a mild change of voice, so read it aloud with Neon's sentence that follows.

### 5) Astra's line as the practical turn before the kicker (Astra, line only; not its recusal thesis)
- **Borrow** (Astra):
  > "Settle who gets to tell you no before you discover how badly you want a yes."
- **Where:** in Neon ¶10, right after "When desires, loyalties, and power are on the line, your intellect is the last witness you should trust uncritically."
- **What it adds:** a concrete thing to *do*, which Neon lacks. It also answers the "using your intellect to decide when to distrust your intellect" circularity: you decide in advance.
- **Cost:** about +16 words.
- **Risks:**
  - Keep it as a single line. Don't import the judge, recusal or AI-launch material.
  - Keep Neon's final sentence as the kicker: "knowing which boundaries were built to save you from yourself". Astra's line should not become the last line.
  - Credit: it is Astra's wording.

### 6) A one-sentence concession on the overclaim (Fable). Optional; do it if item 1 is shortened.
- **Borrow** (Fable):
  > "And intelligence remains, on average, a blessing: people with higher IQs tend to live longer, healthier, more prosperous lives."
- **Where:** in Neon's To-be-sure (¶9), after "Progress sometimes demands defying the consensus of the past."
- **What it adds:** it pre-empts the "IQ correlates with good outcomes" rebuttal, the brain dump's "the opposite appears to be true" overclaim. Neon itself doesn't say "the opposite", but it does say brilliance "often accelerates the wreck."
- **Cost:** about +20 words.
- **Risk:** some duplication with item 1's "as biased as everyone else". Use one of the two if space is tight.

**Not recommended:**

## 2. Factual corrections Neon itself needs

These were rechecked against the earlier factual-risk notes, the two X posts, and AP, Reuters, BBC and NPR (Nov 12–13). The suggested replacements are minimal, and the author's own phrasing is kept where possible.

1. **Huang is now former CEO, and the attribution is off.**
   > Neon: "Consider recent *Washington Post* reporting on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, widely regarded as one of the most formidable strategic minds in modern business."
   - Fix: "…on Jensen Huang, who resigned as Nvidia's CEO on Nov. 12 and was widely regarded as…"
2. **This misattributes the author's wording to the Post and leaves out the "suspected spy" facts.**
   > Neon: "According to the report, Huang engaged in extramarital affairs—including one with a Chinese woman with whom he was doing business."
   - What the Post's post actually says: Huang "resigned as CEO of Nvidia amid reports of two affairs, one of which included a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy."
   - Suggested: "He resigned amid reports of two affairs, one with a former executive of a Singapore firm that bought Nvidia chips, a woman U.S. officials reportedly suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset. China denies it, and the Justice Department says it has reached no conclusion."
   - Facts behind this: her Megaspeed role ran from March to November 2023. Huang admitted being "unfaithful". Do not name the second woman.
3. **Speculation about a private family.**
   > Neon: "Set aside, for a moment, the obvious personal devastation infidelity inflicts on a marriage."
   - Fix: cut it, or use Huang's own apology ("he apologized for hurting his wife and children"). Cutting saves about 15 words.
4. **Fair as opinion, but add the company's position.**
   > Neon: "…entangling romantic indiscretion with a cross-border business relationship invites catastrophic conflict-of-interest scrutiny."
   - The scrutiny is real: there is a Senate Banking Committee request for Dec. 3.
   - Add a short clause, "(Nvidia says it has no reason to believe security or company secrets were compromised)", so the piece doesn't imply harm was proven. About +15 words.
5. **This imputes a mental state as fact.**
   > Neon: "Not because he lacked processing power, but because brilliance combined with invulnerability is a potent sedative."
   - Lower risk. Suggest echoing the author's own hedge, "apparently failed to recognize—or just ignored": "Whatever the reason, it wasn't a lack of processing power."
6. **Wrong company and overstated.**
   > Neon: "Recent reporting on subcultures among staffers at OpenAI—arguably home to some of the most intellectually gifted people alive—highlighted the embrace of practices like 'consensual non-consent.'"
   - What the reporting says (Washington Free Beacon, via Sibarium, Nov. 16):
     - An *Anthropic* researcher is listed as a manager of a Bay Area business hosting "consensual non-consent" parties.
     - The parties reportedly draw a disproportionate number of Anthropic and OpenAI employees.
     - He is not accused of the alleged assault.
   - Suggested: "Recent reporting that an Anthropic researcher helps manage a Bay Area business hosting 'consensual non-consent' parties—events said to draw a disproportionate number of employees from Anthropic and OpenAI, arguably home to some of the most intellectually gifted people alive—…"
   - Keep it unnamed. Don't mention the assault allegation, or if you do, say he isn't accused. Reason disputes the Free Beacon's framing, so "embrace" should go.
7. **Minor: an unsupported trend word.**
   > Neon: "Ultra-clever communities increasingly assume that human psychology can be neatly managed through bespoke rules and contractual cleverness…"
   - It is fine as opinion, and it nicely evokes the parties' safe-word and "default consent" rules. Consider dropping "increasingly". Optional.

**Author's voice to leave alone:**
- "Proceed at your own peril when you pit solo cleverness against the accumulated wisdom built into our cultures and institutions" (from the author's "proceed at your own risk…").
- The domain argument in ¶8, which comes from the author's levels idea.
- The kicker's subordination idea.
- In fix 2, "doing business" is the author's own phrase. If he wants to keep it, keep it in the author's voice and put it in past tense rather than attributing it to the Post.
