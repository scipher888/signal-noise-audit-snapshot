Structure

The long audit separates the essay’s mechanism, its live alternatives, and the places where the evidence stops.

The short version

The argument begins with a cost transfer: generative AI makes plausible submissions cheaper without making judgment cheaper. It then distinguishes work that machines can check from work that still needs human judgment. Where cheap checks exist, strangers can keep entering through the work. Where they do not, institutions have an incentive to substitute prior legibility — records, credentials, referrals, and names.

The audit does not treat that pattern as settled. It carries a balance-sheet alternative, a tooling-lag alternative, seven revision conditions, and a registered court-intake test through roughly the end of 2028.

Canonicality: The essay Signal & Noise stands behind is Issue 22, The Price of Being Read. This is part of its published audit, not a second edition of the essay.

Live alternatives

A

Balance sheet

Rich institutions may automate while small ones lean on names. The registered court test is designed to distinguish this from the gate-type account.

B

Tooling lag

Every queue may be moving through shock, friction, tooling, and reopening. Fast accountable triage would demote the thesis to a temporary lag.

C

Unknowns still get through

If measured first-time entrant throughput holds or rises, the distributional claim fails even if the pile grows and the read rate falls.
