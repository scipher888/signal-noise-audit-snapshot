The Price of Being Read

Twenty-nine source receipts, the exact correction that ran back into the audit, and the line between what the evidence establishes and what the essay infers.

Reference

The receipts establish the examples and boundaries. They do not establish the essay’s forecast.

The short version

The audit carries 29 source receipts across courts, open-source security, hiring, publishing, email, language burden, and detector bias. The strongest evidence establishes the cost and direction of specific queue changes. The weakest part remains the cross-domain forecast that prior reputation will become the cheapest general substitute for human reading.

The correction at the top of the audit matters because the source chain worked in reverse: the author’s later direct read found that the completed audit had understated the review cost and carried a stronger percentage wording than the source actually opened.

Canonicality: The essay Signal & Noise stands behind is Issue 22, The Price of Being Read. This is part of its published audit, not a second edition of the essay.

The inspectable record

The full audited version preserves every receipt with its stated scope and limit. Go directly to the 29-receipt Reference section →

The receipts are the audit’s outside constraints. They are not a vote count for the thesis. Several are self-reports, working papers, dated platform figures, or mirrors used when a primary host could not be reached; those limits remain attached to the claims they bound.

A map, not a certification of truth. These records expose choices, constraints, and open questions. Trust is useful here only when it leads to closer inspection rather than closure.
