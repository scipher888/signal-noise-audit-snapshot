The Price of Being Read

The redline, the full audited argument, its revision conditions, and the 29 source receipts behind the opinion essay.

Published audit

The short essay is the judgment. This package is the inspection surface.

Canonicality: The essay Signal & Noise stands behind is Issue 22, The Price of Being Read. This is part of its published audit, not a second edition of the essay.

Correction added August 1, 2026: This audit is preserved as it stood on July 28 and is not silently rewritten. A direct check of Daniel Stenberg’s July 14, 2025 post resolved “Each.” as referring to each of the three or four security-team members: half an hour to three hours each, or 1.5–12 person-hours per report. The audit’s conservative reading therefore understated the cost by a factor of three to four; the primary essay uses the corrected sentence. The same directly checked post says that “about 5%” of 2025 submissions had proved genuine. This audit’s “below 5%” wording came from a January 2026 announcement that neither pass directly opened. That wording is not being declared false here; it remains unverified. The primary essay therefore uses “at about one in twenty.” The historical text and receipts on the full audited-version page remain unchanged; this note supersedes those two readings.

What this audit can show

It can show

  • Which exact words changed under the Floor Pass.
  • Which sources and limits the longer argument carried.
  • Which contrary outcomes were named before the claim could be tested.

It cannot show

  • That the surviving thesis is correct.
  • That every relevant queue or counterexample was found.
  • That a source receipt is independent simply because it is public.

It did expose

  • A source ambiguity the audit had preserved rather than resolved.
  • A stronger percentage wording tied to a later primary that had not been directly read.
  • A reverse correction in which the short primary became more accurate than its own audit.

Use the four maps

Origin points to the redline. Structure maps the full audited argument. Reference points directly to the receipts. Crosswalk connects each primary paragraph to the audit section that carries its additional burden.

A map, not a certification of truth. These records expose choices, constraints, and open questions. Trust is useful here only when it leads to closer inspection rather than closure.
