The audit's top flags are health framing, not factual error: deliberately engineered over-fullness (H12), about a gallon of water a day concentrated around the last meal (H13), and the claim that off-plan eating "cannot" do much damage (H14). H. Fact-check log

  • H1. Cheesecake. Ultimate Red Velvet Cake Cheesecake is 1,580 kcal (official Cheesecake Factory nutritional guide, Summer 2026). "Roughly 15 miles": at about 100–110 kcal a mile that is 14–16 miles. PASS.
  • H2. NEJM 2011 (Sumithran). At 62 weeks, appetite hormones had not returned to baseline and hunger stayed higher. PASS.
  • H3. Biggest Loser (Fothergill 2016). Six years later, metabolic adaptation was −499 kcal a day. PASS.
  • H4. 2012 study (Hofmann et al., JPSP). High trait self-control went with weaker desires, less conflict and less resistance. PASS.
  • H5. Hall 2019, +508 kcal a day. PASS. The essay's "minimally processed" is the paper's "unprocessed."
  • H6. "Nobody told them to." Participants were told to eat "as much or as little as desired." PRECISION: accurate in spirit.
  • H7. Kidney clearance of 0.8–1 L an hour. Systematic review (735–970 mL an hour) and Noakes 2001 (peak urine output 778 mL an hour). 0.8 L ≈ 3.4 cups and 1 L ≈ 4.2 cups. PASS.
  • H8. GLP-1 drugs "make the stomach feel full." The drugs act mainly through hormonal satiety signaling and slowed gastric emptying. The essay's mechanical analogy is looser than the pharmacology. FACT-SOFT (Stage C, Opus 4.1 #4 and Kimi #2).
  • H9. "Fiber absorbs the water and expands." True of soluble and viscous fibers, not of fiber in general. PRECISION.
  • H10. "Keeps [stretch receptors] activated for hours." Not sourced. FACT-SOFT.
  • H11. "Willpower is an unreliable resource, especially at the end of a long day." Opinion. v3 softened it from v2's "finite resource" because the ego-depletion literature replicates poorly. FACT-SOFT.
  • H12. HEALTH: engineered over-fullness as the goal ("borderline-excessive fullness"; the author's stomach-turn line). It is the author's stated experience. The essay names eating disorders in its caveat. Flag for the audit page.
  • H13. HEALTH: about a gallon a day, mostly during and after the last meal. It is framed as the author's practice "not a prescription," followed by the sourced clearance ceiling and a physician caution. Stage C (Kimi #7) notes that compressing most of a gallon into a few hours can approach the ceiling. Flag.
  • H14. HEALTH: "you simply cannot do the kind of caloric damage" when off-plan; "Your body self-corrects." Overstated. Flag (Stage C, Kimi #11).
  • H15. Nuts and seeds listed as high volume "relative to their caloric load." They are energy-dense. PRECISION (Stage C, Kimi #10).
  • H16. GBD and Greger's Daily Dozen. Named as frameworks, with no figures attached. PASS.

I. Internal consistency

  • I1. The essay rejects calorie counting, then says "do the math once … 2,200 calories." It frames this as a one-time step, but both Stage C legs flag it.
  • I2. The title promises to "Stuff It Into Submission," while the body ends on "it feels like immunity," a felt experience. That is consistent with the author's ruling.

Objections that land

  1. Willpower is displaced, not removed (Opus 1, Kimi 12). Over-stuffing, a 3 p.m. cutoff and a gallon of water need discipline too. The closer ("stop being asked") promises more than the protocol delivers. LANDS (convergent).
  2. "Do the math once, 2,200 calories" sits awkwardly with rejecting counting (Kimi 5, Opus 7). LANDS (convergent). The essay frames it as a one-time step, but readers may not see the difference.
  3. GLP-1 analogy: drug satiety is hormonal, not stomach stretch (Kimi 2, Opus 4). LANDS (convergent). This matches audit H8.
  4. Engineered over-fullness flirts with the binge/restrict psychology it rejects; the eating-disorder caveat is thinner than the behavior praised (Kimi 1). LANDS. This matches audit H12 and is the most important health-framing note.
  5. Water timing: compressing most of a gallon into and after the last meal can approach the very ceiling the essay cites (Kimi 7). LANDS. This matches audit H13.
  6. "You simply cannot do the kind of caloric damage … your body self-corrects" overpromises, against the essay's own Hall evidence (Kimi 11). LANDS. This matches audit H14.
  7. Restriction evidence comes from extreme cases (Biggest Loser, very-low-energy diet; Opus 2, Kimi 6). LANDS (convergent).
  8. Hall shows that environment changes intake, not that this protocol works (Kimi 3). LANDS.
  9. Partly lands: the opening false binary (Opus 3); the 3 p.m. cutoff and six-hour buffer drift from "I" to "you" (Kimi 8); social cost dismissed as a "minor eccentricity" (Opus 6); habituation to stretch (Kimi 9); nuts and seeds are energy-dense (Kimi 10, HYGIENE).

Misread log: Opus 4.1 #5 says the 2012 self-control study undercuts the anti-willpower thesis. It doesn't. The study finds that high self-control works by meeting fewer temptations rather than resisting more, which is the essay's point. Kimi #4's reverse-causation note is fair but overreaches: the essay says "the skill worth copying," not that stuffing creates the trait.

Signal & Noise Issue 35: sources for the human/AI essay (08-argon-core-v2)

Checked October 31, 2026 (PT).

  1. Cheesecake calories. The Cheesecake Factory Nutritional Guide, Summer 2026 (official): https://www.thecheesecakefactory.com/media/631/download. Ultimate Red Velvet Cake Cheesecake is 1,580 kcal per slice. For comparison: Original 830, Oreo Dream Extreme 1,510, Brownie Crunch Choc-A-Lot 1,500. Draft verdict: Argon's "easily clocks in at 1,500" was true only for the richest slices, so the essay now names the slice.
  2. Running cost. Margaria et al., "Energy cost of running," J Appl Physiol 1963;18(2):367–370, https://doi.org/10.1152/jappl.1963.18.2.367. Net cost is about 1 kcal/kg/km, which is about 110 kcal/mile for a 70 kg (154 lb) adult. Also an abstract in Med Sci Sports Exerc 2017 that tested the "100 kcal/mile" estimate (measured 108.6 ± 31.5): https://doi.org/10.1249/01.mss.0000517366.87924.fa. 1,580 kcal works out to about 14–16 miles, so "roughly 15 miles" holds.
  3. Sumithran et al., NEJM 2011;365:1597–1604. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22029981/. 50 adults lost 13.5 kg on a 10-week very-low-energy diet. At 62 weeks, leptin, PYY, CCK and insulin were still lower, ghrelin, GIP and pancreatic polypeptide were still higher, and hunger was still higher than at baseline. Draft verdict: "hunger hormones remained elevated" was imprecise, so it was reworded.
  4. Fothergill et al., Obesity 2016;24(8):1612–1619. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27136388/. 14 contestants were measured 6 years later. RMR was 704 kcal/day below baseline, and metabolic adaptation (adjusted for body composition and age) was −499 ± 207 kcal/day. Draft verdict: holds, with "at rest ... for their size" added.
  5. Hofmann, Baumeister, Förster & Vohs, JPSP 2012;102(6):1318–1335. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22149456/ ; full text: https://assets.csom.umn.edu/assets/lib/assets/AssetLibrary/2012/Hofmann_Baumeister_Forster_Vohs_2012_JPSP.pdf. 205 adults filed 7,827 desire reports by beeper. High trait self-control was associated with weaker desires, less conflict and lower rates of resistance. The authors conclude that high self-control "operates more by avoiding temptations in the first place than by resisting them." Draft verdict: the claim holds, and it was reworded to match.
  6. Hall et al., Cell Metabolism 2019;30(1):67–77. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31105044/. 20 inpatients were instructed to eat "as much or as little as desired" and ate 508 ± 106 kcal/day more on the ultra-processed diet. They gained 0.9 kg on it and lost 0.9 kg on the unprocessed diet. Draft verdict: holds.
  7. Water: kidney excretion ceiling. - Joo et al., systematic review, "Clinical characteristics and outcomes of hyponatraemia associated with oral water intake in adults," https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8663108/. Water intoxication occurs when intake "exceeds the capacity of the kidney to excrete water (0.8–1.0 L per hour)," and maximal excretion is 735–970 mL/hour. - Noakes et al., "Peak rates of diuresis in healthy humans during oral fluid overload," S Afr Med J 2001, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11732457/. Peak urine output was 778 ± 39 mL/h, and capacity was limited above about 900 mL/h. - Conversion: 0.8 L ≈ 3.4 US cups, 1.0 L ≈ 4.2 cups. - The ceiling is lower with low solute intake or reduced kidney function: Hyponatremia Demystified, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9993811/. - The US military hot-weather cap (~1–1.5 qt/hour, ~12 qt/day) was not verified from a primary source this round and is not used in the essay.
