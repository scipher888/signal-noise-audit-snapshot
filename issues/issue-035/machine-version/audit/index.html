A. Fact-check log

  • A1. "half a kilogram and a kilogram a year." The essay attributes this to unnamed "large studies." Its own next sentence (Mozaffarian, 3.35 lb per 4 years) works out to about 0.38 kg a year, below that range. FACT-SOFT (see B1).
  • A2. "more than 120,000 nurses and other health professionals … about three and a third pounds every four years." Mozaffarian et al., NEJM 2011: 120,877 adults in three cohorts, an average gain of 3.35 lb per 4-year period. PASS.
  • A3. Hall 2019. The meals were matched for presented calories, energy density, macronutrients, sugar, sodium and fiber. Intake was 508 kcal a day higher on the ultra-processed diet, eating rate was faster, and participants gained 0.9 kg on it and lost 0.9 kg on the unprocessed diet. "Minimally processed" is the paper's "unprocessed." PRECISION: the figures hold, but see B2.
  • A4. Foods most associated with gain (potato chips, potatoes, sugar-sweetened drinks, processed and red meats) and with less gain (vegetables, whole grains, fruit, nuts, yogurt). These match Mozaffarian 2011 exactly. PASS.
  • A5. Liquid calories that appetite "barely registers." This is a common reading of the compensation literature. The essay cites no study for it. Logged, not tested further.
  • A6. "A thirty-minute jog burns roughly what a large bakery muffin contains." At roughly 100 kcal a mile, a 30-minute jog of about 3 miles is around 300 kcal. Large bakery muffins often run higher than that. PRECISION; the direction holds.
  • A7. Pontzer, constrained energy expenditure. The body partly offsets higher activity. PASS.
  • A8. National Weight Control Registry. Members average about an hour of activity a day, and walking is the most common activity. PASS (well-documented registry finding; no primary source fetched this round).
  • A9. "That erosion [muscle loss] is one reason weight creeps up even when habits stay the same." Plausible but asserted. FACT-SOFT.
  • A10. Tasali et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2022. 80 adults with overweight who habitually slept under 6.5 hours. Sleep rose about 1.2 hours a night and intake fell 270 kcal a day against control, over 2 weeks. The essay omits that the sample was adults with overweight and that the trial lasted two weeks. PRECISION.
  • A11. SNAP, Wing et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2016. 599 adults aged 18–35, mean follow-up 3 years. Weight change was +0.26 kg (control), −0.56 kg (small changes) and −2.37 kg (large changes, which deliberately lost 2.3–4.5 kg up front). "Held steady or lost a little" holds. PASS.
  • A12. Holiday gain of "less than a pound … not lost afterward." This matches the classic Yanovski et al. finding (NEJM 2000, about 0.37 kg). PASS (no primary source fetched this round).
  • A13. Medications, thyroid disease and sleep apnea as causes of gain. PASS (standard clinical list).
  • A14. "Most of the weight they take off eventually returns." Broadly supported. The essay gives no figure. PASS.

B. Internal consistency

Objections that land

  1. The Hall trial doesn't support the mechanism drawn from it (Kimi 2). The meals were matched for fiber, so "fullness per calorie" can't explain the 500 extra calories. The design points to eating speed and palatability instead. LANDS. This matches audit B2.
  2. The opening numbers don't reconcile (Kimi 8). 3⅓ lb in 4 years is about 0.38 kg a year, below the stated range. LANDS (HYGIENE). This matches audit B1.
  3. Loss-side evidence used for prevention (Kimi 4, Opus 6). The National Weight Control Registry is made up of people who lost weight and kept it off, while the essay's premise is that prevention differs from loss. LANDS (convergent).
  4. Two-week trials carry a twenty-year argument (Kimi 6, Opus 2/4). LANDS.
  5. Two "only one thing" levers collide (Kimi 3: drinks versus bedtime). LANDS, low severity.
  6. The closing silt metaphor inverts the thesis (Kimi 1). "Clearing the channel" reads as removal, not prevention. Partly lands.
  7. The 2–3 lb trigger sits inside the noise band the essay just described (Kimi 9). Partly lands.
  8. SNAP was a bundle, not just the scale (Kimi 7). Partly lands.
  9. Lower weight: observational limits of Mozaffarian (Opus 1); the prevention/diet distinction is mostly definitional (Kimi 5); "hyper-palatable" is circular (Opus 5); the exercise asymmetry is unexplained (Opus 3; the essay partly answers it via Pontzer).
