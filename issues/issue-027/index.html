Source checked

This dual-audit was a source check against the named research and a floor pass. The Goodsell–Thornley competition is subject context for the live test, not a claim that either essay is an entry. It was not a three-company document audit.

  • FunSearch — Romera-Paredes et al., Mathematical discoveries from program search with large language models, Nature 625, 468–475 (2024)
  • DeepMind, FunSearch: Making new discoveries in mathematical sciences using Large Language Models
  • SwarmWorld — Pal, Wang, and Buehler, arXiv:2608.26081 (26 August 2026)
  • AI Philosophy Competition, 1st Edition — Goodsell and Thornley, zacharygoodsell.com/ai-philosophy-competition (subject context)

Objections raised, and what happened to each

Floor objections against the author freeze: none that failed. Stage C ran two legs — moonshotai/kimi-k3 and anthropic/claude-opus-4.1 (stealth/ox-alpha was unavailable). The legs converged on four argument-scope objections. Those publish here under ruling A; they do not rewrite the landing. One additional claim was raised as if it were a floor fact and did not land. Dispositions summarize the objections; they do not invent Stage C quotations.

  1. FunSearch evaluator gap versus philosophy. The essay uses a system whose novelty was checked by an automated evaluator, then bets that a related logic can organize philosophical inquiry, which has no comparable scoring function. LANDS as audit-only. Argument-scope. The source check already treats FunSearch as verified mathematical novelty, not as a demonstrated philosophical result.
  2. “Already here” versus a bet. Capacity-now and the FunSearch-to-philosophy transfer are bets, not completed demonstrations. LANDS as audit-only. Opinion-scope, same lossy-compression ruling as Issues 23 and 25.
  3. Originality and contribution under-specified. What would count as a new contribution — and how a reader would know one had arrived — is thinner than the title’s confidence. LANDS as audit-only.
  4. Human steering versus machine origin. The author originated and steered the thesis; ChatGPT-6/Astra drafted most of the prose. That split is a live question about who originated the contribution. LANDS as audit-only. The development record is where that split is visible; ruling A does not rewrite the essay to advertise it.
  5. “August 2026 is the future,” raised as floor. One Stage C leg treated an August 2026 SwarmWorld date as a calendar impossibility. DID NOT LAND. Instrument/calendar error. SwarmWorld, arXiv:2608.26081, is dated 26 August 2026. The dateline of this audit is 6 September 2026.

The dual gap, stated so the two landings are not merged: the author essay bets on now-capacity and on cumulative shared inquiry. The machine companion bets on author-neutral originality in principle, and treats the competition as a test of that claim. Those are different theses.

Floor and what matched

Floor fails on the author essay: 0. The FunSearch claims match the Nature paper and the DeepMind blog: a language model paired with an automated evaluator produced new cap-set constructions beyond previously known results. The SwarmWorld claims match arXiv:2608.26081. Opinion-scope sentences — “already here,” and the transfer from FunSearch to philosophy — are bets, not floor facts.

There is no accepted floor row and therefore no wording on the human redline. The landing is the freeze. This page is where Stage C argument objections are visible, so the author does not get credit for silent improvements and the audit does not pretend those objections were never raised.

What ran

Author-essay drafting: ChatGPT-6/Astra, author-steered, in the two public shares on the development record. Machine companion: openai/gpt-6-astra-pro via OpenRouter at reasoning effort xhigh, seeded with the Goodsell competition and a brainstorm only, locked before the author upload. Floor and source check: already run before this package. Stage C critique legs: moonshotai/kimi-k3 and anthropic/claude-opus-4.1. Ox Alpha was unavailable.

What this audit did: check the author essay against FunSearch and SwarmWorld, record 0 floor fails, and publish Stage C argument objections with dispositions. What it did not do: change freeze wording, treat the machine companion as a second edition, or merge the two landings.
