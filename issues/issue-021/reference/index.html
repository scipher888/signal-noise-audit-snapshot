Delivered, Then Invisible

Eight outside dependencies, four corrections that ran against the essay, and the places where the sources stop and the argument starts.

Reference

Every correction the sources forced made the essay’s case harder to argue, not easier.

The short version

The reference layer groups the issue’s sources into eight dependencies: the prediction and how it developed; energy at civilizational scale; materials and processing; autonomous medicine; the delivery record; the price of positional advantage; distribution and the one planetary precedent; and income, attention, and rank.

Four figures were corrected by going back to the originals, and all four ran against the essay. A school premium was cut from 2.5 to 2.1 percent because the higher number is the paper’s abstract and matches no specification inside it. A medical comparison was rebuilt on career stage rather than specialty, which is what the paper actually says and which narrows the gap the essay was claiming. A robot-mass figure was restated as an all-steel upper bound, overstating the burden the essay then clears. A solar price series was narrowed to the two endpoints its source actually carries, retiring a larger and more impressive ratio.

None of this certifies the landing. The sources establish that the scale is not thermodynamically forbidden, that expertise is becoming copyable, that delivery has already moved the floor once, and that rank tracks mortality. Sorting goods into three layers, and wagering on where attention goes next, remain the essay’s own.

What the references did

Changed

  • Corrected a school-premium figure from 2.5 to 2.1 percent, and identified the higher number as an abstract that no specification in its own paper supports.
  • Rebuilt a medical comparison on the paper’s actual arms — trainees and residents against everyone past that stage — rather than the press release’s specialty framing.
  • Restated eight billion robots at 100 kilograms as total robot mass, carried as an all-steel upper bound rather than as steel.
  • Narrowed a solar price fall to the 1977 and 2014 endpoints the cited source carries, and dropped a larger ratio that depended on an endpoint the page does not contain.

Narrowed

  • The mortality-gradient study never measured, stratified by, or adjusted for income; grade and salary move together in that workforce, so it cannot show rank biting at a fixed income.
  • The autonomous surgical result covers one phase, on dead tissue outside the body, with a human reloading clips between steps and no dissection attempted.
  • The eradication vote arithmetic rests on a single official history, and the apparently independent corroborations trace back to that same book.
  • The poverty figures use one purchasing-power series, and the essay states a ratio rather than endpoints because two of the publisher’s own surfaces differ on them.

Killed or not imported

  • No reach or engagement figure for the original post. No press source, archive, or snapshot carries one, and no public metric measures reading.
  • No reserves-to-production countdown for rare earths. Reserves are a commercial inventory, not a clock.
  • No claim that the search was exhaustive. Where the essay reports an absence, it is scoped to the appearances actually searched.
  • No rate claim that new scarcity regenerates faster than technology dissolves it. The cited work supports a shift in the mix and nothing faster.

Source ledger

RL-021-01: The prediction, and how it developed.

Why it matteredThe essay refuses to audit the weakest version. A 2025 post with no payer and no date became, by 2026, a named institution and a named year, and the improved forecast is the one tested.

Public sourcesThe August 2025 post, verified by verbatim press reproduction and by decoding the post identifier’s embedded timestamp; an April 2026 post naming federal checks; a July 2026 broadcast interview, resting on same-day press reproduction and a third-party transcript; and a April 2026 exchange about penthouses.

ScopeEstablishes what was said and when. It does not establish reach, sincerity, or commitment — and the essay flags that “should issue checks” is advocacy, not forecast.

RL-021-02: Energy at civilizational scale, and the waste-heat ceiling.

Why it matteredThe first leg needs a defensible number for what eight billion people at rich-country rates would draw, and a ceiling above it.

Public sourcesPer-person energy data on one substitution method; a hand calculation from no-feedback climate sensitivity, corroborated by two published treatments of waste heat as a growth limit.

ScopeSupports the absence of an aggregate thermodynamic contradiction and locates the heat ceiling far above the requirement. It does not price the transition, the factories, the water, or any local bottleneck, and one accounting method is used throughout.

RL-021-03: Materials, and where the bottleneck actually sits.

Why it matteredA humanoid fleet at scale invites an obvious materials objection, and the essay wanted the honest version of it.

Public sourcesWorld crude-steel output; crustal abundance and production-concentration figures from the official minerals surveys; a congressional research summary on separation capacity.

ScopeSupports that the constraint is mostly built rather than mostly geological, while carrying three caveats that cut the other way: minable concentrations are less common than for most commodities, separation is chemically hard, and the heavy rare earths are an order of magnitude scarcer.

RL-021-04: Autonomous medicine, and where it actually stands.

Why it matteredMedicine is the good the whole prediction leans on hardest, so the essay needed both the real frontier and the real gap.

Public sourcesAn autonomous diagnostic authorized in 2018 and its pivotal-trial figures; a 2025 autonomous surgical-phase result; and a 2025 meta-analysis of diagnostic accuracy across 83 studies.

ScopeSupports genuine autonomy in a narrow diagnostic task and a real surgical-phase demonstration. It does not support “a robot performed surgery.” The meta-analysis measures accuracy on written vignettes and image sets, not clinical practice, from a 52.1 percent pooled base — and its arms are career stage, not specialty.

RL-021-05: The delivery record, and what barely moved.

Why it matteredThe claim that the delivery engine is measured rather than hoped needs measurements, including the ones that embarrass it.

Public sourcesA solar module price history at its two carried endpoints; World Bank poverty and income-line series on one purchasing-power basis.

ScopeSupports a large fall in extreme poverty and a genuine rise in the middle. It also carries the correction that an earlier draft denied the middle rose, and the fact that the share below the top prosperity line moved under five points in twenty-nine years.

RL-021-06: What positional advantage costs, where it is priced.

Why it matteredThe residue needs at least one place where relative standing is measured in money rather than asserted.

Public sourcesAn attendance-boundary study of suburban Boston house prices; a university’s own published admissions data across two class years.

ScopeSupports that relative school quality is capitalised into land, at about 2.1 percent per five percent higher scores on a metric that is itself scored relative at state level. The admissions figures support a demand surge consistent with rank-demand; they carry applications and offers, not motives, and the offer count is a decision people make.

RL-021-07: Distribution, lag, and the one planetary precedent.

Why it matteredThe third leg turns on whether large-scale delivery has ever been decided and kept decided.

Public sourcesMill on production and distribution, with his own hedge; an economic history of Britain 1780–1900; the World Health Organization’s official eradication history; and the 1980 resolution that closed the programme.

ScopeSupports that distribution is institutional rather than physical, that wage lag is real and finite, and that the one planetary success was authorised by very few people. The resolution vests nothing in anyone, which is the essay’s point. The vote arithmetic has a single-source dependency, disclosed.

RL-021-08: Income, attention, and rank.

Why it matteredThe wager needs its welfare premise granted, its mechanism motivated, and its stakes shown.

Public sourcesCross-country work on income and subjective well-being, with its standing counter-literature; a 1976 treatment of positional goods; a 1971 essay on attention scarcity; and a twenty-five-year mortality follow-up of British civil servants.

ScopeSupports that well-being and income rise together, that the positional share of competition grows as material needs are met, that attention is finite, and that a rank gradient tracks mortality. None of them shows that attention moves the way this essay expects. That step is the wager, and it is labelled as one.

A map, not a certification of truth. These records expose choices, constraints, and open questions. Trust is useful here only when it leads to closer inspection rather than closure.
