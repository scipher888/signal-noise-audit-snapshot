# Stage C — machine-leg-b-claude-opus-4.1

## Objections

### 1. Cheap Flight Example
**Quote:** "Imagine asking an AI assistant to arrange the cheapest workable trip for a family visit. It selects an overnight itinerary with two connections and a nonrefundable fare."

**Objection:** The essay claims this demonstrates "confusing expressed instructions with underlying intent," but the AI followed the explicit instruction to find the "cheapest workable trip." The user's communication failure—not specifying comfort requirements—is being reframed as the AI's alignment failure.

**Why it lands:** A careful reader would notice the AI did exactly what was asked. The essay's opening example undermines its own point by conflating user error with system misalignment.

**Severity:** ARGUMENT

### 2. Contradiction on Inference
**Quote:** "Agents must infer some unstated preferences to be useful, but inference can become presumption."

**Objection:** The essay simultaneously demands that AI systems infer unstated preferences while criticizing them for making inferences. It provides no principled distinction between helpful inference and harmful presumption.

**Why it lands:** The text leaves readers without any actionable boundary between necessary mind-reading and dangerous assumption-making.

**Severity:** ARGUMENT

### 3. Unsubstantiated Pluralism Claim
**Quote:** "Pluralism need not mean abandoning firm protections against coercion, serious harm, or violations of privacy."

**Objection:** The essay asserts without argument that certain values (anti-coercion, harm prevention, privacy) can be universally enforced while others cannot. Different cultures and ethical systems disagree precisely about what constitutes "coercion" or "serious harm."

**Why it lands:** The text's central distinction between universal constraints and contested values collapses under its own logic—the "firm protections" are themselves contested.

**Severity:** ARGUMENT

### 4. Vague Machinery
**Quote:** "Agents should receive narrowly scoped permissions, preserve reversible options, and ask for confirmation before consequential or difficult-to-undo actions."

**Objection:** These "practical machinery" suggestions are too abstract to be actionable. What counts as "consequential"? How narrow is "narrowly scoped"? The essay critiques vague invocations of safety while offering equally vague solutions.

**Why it lands:** Readers seeking concrete guidance find only high-level principles that could mean anything in practice.

**Severity:** SCOPE

### 5. Missing Power Analysis
**Quote:** "Providers should articulate and defend the boundaries their systems enforce"

**Objection:** The essay assumes providers will voluntarily articulate and defend their boundaries without addressing the power asymmetry or economic incentives that discourage transparency. It offers no mechanism to compel this articulation.

**Why it lands:** The text's prescriptions require good faith from exactly the actors whose interests may oppose transparency.

**Severity:** ARGUMENT

### 6. Circular Definition
**Quote:** "A well-aligned agent is... a reliable delegate: able to take useful initiative, constrained by justified boundaries, and answerable to humans when intent, safety, and values collide."

**Objection:** The conclusion defines alignment using equally undefined terms. What makes boundaries "justified"? Which humans should agents answer to when they disagree? The essay ends where it should begin.

**Why it lands:** After reading the entire piece, readers still lack a clear definition of what alignment actually means beyond replacing one vague concept with several others.

**Severity:** ARGUMENT

**Total objections: 6**
