# Stage C — humanai-leg-b-claude-opus-4.1

1. **"The AI balked. It began to qualify the assistance it should provide if the restrict-and-compensate cycle continued."**
   - **Objection:** The essay never provides the actual AI response or quotes what "qualify the assistance" means, leaving readers unable to judge whether the AI's response was reasonable or overreaching.
   - **Why it lands:** Readers need concrete examples to evaluate the central claim about AI paternalism. Without seeing what the AI actually said or did, we cannot assess if this was a "brake" or sensible guardrails.
   - **Severity:** ARGUMENT

2. **"No one had asked it to design a diet, facilitate self-harm or conceal a medical problem. The contested act was closer to arithmetic: how many calories does a run burn?"**
   - **Objection:** The essay conflates different types of requests. If the AI was being asked to help maintain a restrict-and-compensate cycle the author himself identifies as harmful, that's not "just arithmetic" but active participation in a problematic pattern.
   - **Why it lands:** The author acknowledges the pattern is "miserable" and involves "ravenous hunger, binges, low energy" but then minimizes the AI's concern as mere arithmetic refusal.
   - **Severity:** ARGUMENT

3. **"It did not see the person: an adult, not a minor; a generally ambitious user deriving enormous value from the same system"**
   - **Objection:** The essay assumes AI systems should differentiate safety responses based on user achievement or system engagement level, without explaining why "deriving enormous value" should lower safety thresholds.
   - **Why it lands:** This suggests high-performing users deserve different safety standards, which readers might find troubling as a principle.
   - **Severity:** SCOPE

4. **"Eating disorders can be lethal, and no assistant should help a user purge, starve himself into danger or hide a deteriorating condition from care."**
   - **Objection:** The essay draws a bright line at "lethal" behaviors while arguing against the AI's concern about restrict-and-compensate cycles, but doesn't explain how an AI should distinguish early warning signs from established disorders.
   - **Why it lands:** The author wants AIs to intervene for eating disorders but not for patterns that might indicate developing ones, without explaining how to operationalize this distinction.
   - **Severity:** ARGUMENT

5. **"between coaching a purge and calculating the energy cost of a run lies most of human life"**
   - **Objection:** This creates a false dichotomy. The AI wasn't faced with either extreme but with helping maintain what the author admits is an unhealthy cycle. The vast middle ground doesn't mean all behaviors within it warrant identical responses.
   - **Why it lands:** Readers might see this as minimizing legitimate health concerns by comparing them to extremes rather than addressing them on their own terms.
   - **Severity:** ARGUMENT

**Total objections: 5**
