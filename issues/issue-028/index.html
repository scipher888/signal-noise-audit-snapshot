Source checked

This dual-audit was a source check against the named literature and a floor pass. It was not a three-company document audit.

  • Mark Zuckerberg, Open Source AI Is the Path Forward, Meta, 23 July 2024
  • Qi et al., Fine-tuning Aligned Language Models Compromises Safety, Even When Users Do Not Intend To!, arXiv:2310.03693 (2023)
  • International AI Safety Report 2026, Executive Summary

Objections raised, and what happened to each

Floor objections against the author freeze: none that failed. Stage C ran two legs — moonshotai/kimi-k3 and anthropic/claude-opus-4.1 (stealth/ox-alpha was unavailable). The legs converged on three argument-scope objections. Those publish here under ruling A; they do not rewrite the landing. Dispositions summarize the objections; they do not invent Stage C quotations.

  1. Weaker “equal intelligence” slogan versus the cited case. The essay hits a weaker equal-intelligence claim. The Zuckerberg-style case also claims institutional resource advantages the essay partly concedes. LANDS as audit-only. Argument-scope.
  2. Foreign-release concession versus the release test. The concession that foreign release can already put weights in circulation is not integrated into the release test, or into the marginal harm of another domestic release. LANDS as audit-only. Scope.
  3. Weighing standard missing. The claim that defensive benefits outweigh additional harm, and the staged-access recommendation, lack an assessable weighing standard. LANDS as audit-only. Argument-scope.

The dual gap, stated so the two landings are not merged: the author essay argues from structural attacker–defender asymmetries and cited literature. The machine companion argues that recognition is not jurisdiction, and that stop-power is not established by equal download. Those are different theses.

Floor and what matched

Floor fails on the author essay: 0. Named facts matched the fetched sources. Zuckerberg 2024: as long as everyone has access to similar generations of models — which open source promotes — then governments and institutions with more compute resources will be able to check bad actors with less compute; also pre-release testing, mitigation, and red-teaming. Qi et al. 2023: fine-tuning can compromise safety alignment even without malicious intent. International AI Safety Report 2026 executive summary: whether attackers or defenders will benefit more from AI assistance remains uncertain; open-weight models cannot be recalled once released; safeguards are easier to remove.

There is no accepted floor row and therefore no wording on the human redline. The landing is the freeze. This page is where Stage C argument objections are visible, so the author does not get credit for silent improvements and the audit does not pretend those objections were never raised.
