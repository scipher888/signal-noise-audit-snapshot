## 0. Purpose

Signal & Noise exists to reduce self-deception while making ideas usable.

**Core rule:** editorial usefulness may simplify expression, but may never override technical validity at the load-bearing layer.

The publication is not trying to present certainty. It is trying to publish disciplined updates under uncertainty.

## 1. Two-layer architecture

### Layer A — truth layer

Claims must be typed before they carry weight.

- **Observation** — what was seen.
- **Interpretation** — what it might mean.
- **Hypothesis** — a testable mechanism.
- **Recommendation** — what to do.

Every framework-level hypothesis must include:

1. disconfirmers — what would prove this wrong;
2. prediction — what should happen if true;
3. time horizon — when it can be checked;
4. alternative hypotheses — at least two.

If a claim lacks disconfirmers, it cannot be framed as explanatory truth.

### Layer B — narrative layer

Language may be simplified, but uncertainty must remain visible. Storytelling is allowed; overclaiming is not. Nuance removed for readability must remain inspectable in the issue's public record.

**Opinion Piece scope:** Layer A binds the published audit rather than the primary essay. The primary essay is bound by the Floor Pass in §5. The redline and audited version provide the full-scale record of removed or restored nuance.

## 2. Publication modes

Each issue declares one primary mode.

1. **Explainer** — claims causal understanding; highest burden.
2. **Field Notes** — reports observations plus provisional interpretation.
3. **Reflection** — values and meaning-making, not causal authority.
4. **Opinion Piece** — presents the author's synthesized judgment as such; floor-gated, linked to a published audit in The World Behind the Words.

Default mode: **Field Notes**, unless the Explainer burden is met. Opinion Piece is an explicit author-level lane choice, not a default.

## 3. Landing selection for non-opinion issues

For Explainers, Field Notes, and Reflections, the landing is selected rather than silently synthesized around a prior.

1. The issue begins with a question, not a thesis.
2. Reader stakes, research, and the strongest fair case for each major live position are developed before a landing or narrative arc exists.
3. A Landing Slate presents two to four genuine rival landings, including the strongest defensible asymmetric landing in each live direction.
4. A symmetric or unsettled landing must name either primary-verifiable parity evidence or the specific missing evidence that would decide the issue. A hedge does not earn survival merely by asserting little.
5. Each candidate is tested for what it is willing to be wrong about, what a smart critic would deny, what practical judgment would change, what its cited anchors actually support, and what evidence would kill it.
6. The human author selects the landing and owns that decision. The author may choose a candidate that did not fare best in review or a landing that was not in the slate, provided the authored decision is recorded.
7. Only after selection is the narrative arc built. The arc may not silently choose a different landing.

Criticism reports; it does not rank or validate. A landing that survives criticism is a survivor, not a proven truth.

### Protected reader relevance and fair opposition

When moral salience, practical stakes, personal tension, or public consequence is load-bearing, the issue identifies those elements before the landing exists. AI review may challenge, narrow, source, or recommend changes to them, but it may not silently remove or rewrite them. Substantive removal or modification belongs to the human author.

For moralized, partisan, identity- or status-coded, or otherwise prior-triggering issues, the process must state the strongest emotionally legible case for each major live position in terms serious advocates would recognize as fair. This is not false equivalence: a fair statement can still be weaker empirically, legally, morally, or practically.

## 4. Required editorial roles

No issue should publish unless the roles applicable to its declared mode have run.

### Builder

Drafts the thesis and supporting structure. May optimize for usefulness and readability, but may not hide uncertainty to make the draft feel finished or weaken a contestable thesis until it becomes trivial.

### Outside adversarial review

Pressure-tests a complete draft for thesis, evidence, scope, alternatives, landing drift, and whether criticism made the essay merely safer rather than stronger. Outside output is input, not authority.

### Critic / Adversary

Attacks assumptions, mechanism, evidence, alternatives, and scope using the Core Adversarial Battery.

### Referee

Labels claims and publication risk:

- keep;
- qualify;
- downgrade confidence or publication mode;
- overreach;
- removal recommended;
- fact-check before publication;
- no-publish / rework recommended.

The Referee controls epistemic labels, confidence, source requirements, and publication recommendations. The human author decides final wording, removal, retention, revision, publication, and whether to proceed despite a warning. When the author proceeds despite a warning, that decision is recorded.

### Editor

Produces readable final prose while preserving Referee constraints. Editor may compress, sequence, and clarify. Editor may not upgrade confidence, remove hedges, weaken warrants, silently drop load-bearing caveats, erase protected reader-relevance material, or homogenize a sharp defensible judgment into generic prose.

### External polish

External polish may improve prose quality only: cohesiveness, rhythm, readability, and instant comprehension. It may not add claims, upgrade confidence, remove caveats, or alter transparency language. If polish introduces a substantive issue, it routes back through Referee.

**Opinion Piece scope:** the Landing Slate and selector do not run. The author's judgment selects the thesis openly. The adversarial roles run in the published audit; the Referee also rules at the prepublication Floor Pass.

## 5. Hard publish / no-publish gates

Every issue passes an accuracy and fairness floor before publication. Every issue is audited claim by claim, and the audit — including what it weakens — is published in The World Behind the Words. The audit is an account of flaws and limits, not proof that the issue is right.

### Non-opinion issues

A non-opinion issue receives a **no-publish / author decision required** label if any of the following are true:

- a framework claim is unfalsifiable but written as explanatory truth;
- no credible alternative hypotheses were considered;
- narrative confidence exceeds evidentiary confidence;
- the piece can explain any outcome;
- the central argument survives only by becoming tautological or self-evident;
- the work depends on process claims readers cannot inspect;
- the work depends on personhood ambiguity around the AI process.

### Opinion Piece Floor Pass

An Opinion Piece's primary essay cannot publish unless all seven Floor Pass requirements are met. The pass is recorded claim by claim, with the Referee ruling at the floor.

1. **Accuracy:** figures are exact and quotations are verbatim, verified against primary sources at pass time. A paper's underlying specifications take priority over its abstract, press release, or other summary layer.
2. **Named-person fairness:** claims about a named person's views, motives, or conduct are anchored or clearly framed as the author's account of how the conduct lands.
3. **Receipt or cut:** unquantified-frequency claims and claims that something does not exist require support or removal.
4. **Calibration:** statistically qualified quantitative findings keep the qualifier in plain language or are restated below the evidence's ceiling.
5. **Identification:** the issue is identified as an Opinion Piece where readers arrive — subject line, archive listing, and on-page standfirst — without repeated in-text status marking.
6. **Integrity:** the essay is coherent, borrows no process claims it did not earn, and corrections extend rather than silently rewrite the public record.
7. **No empty hedging:** a hedge remains only when it carries real epistemic weight.

The Floor Pass is the Opinion Piece's prepublication no-publish gate. The full confidence, falsifiability, alternatives, and claim-typing gates bind the published audit.

### Audit coupling

Every Opinion Piece links to its claim-by-claim audit in The World Behind the Words. The audit is less a claim to epistemic validity than a public record of flaws, limits, and what the evidence weakens.

- The primary issue displays an audit-status line: audit pending, with its due status; or audit complete, with a link.
- Floor-class findings in the audit flow back through the corrections regime.
- If the audit refutes the thesis, the author must choose among a standfirst amendment, re-labeling, or retraction.
- **The audit for issue N must be live before issue N+1 ships.**

The audit creates an inspectable record. It does not prove the primary judgment correct.

## 6. Core adversarial battery

The Critic must address all five questions.

1. What is this model designed to miss?
2. What would disconfirm this?
3. What are at least two alternative explanations?
4. What is this framework optimized to make invisible?
5. What would need to be true for the opposite conclusion to be correct?

The point is not to perform skepticism. The point is to make the draft answer to something other than its own coherence.

The list is capped at five. Adding a question requires retiring one.

## 7. Claim ledger and public corrections

Signal & Noise maintains an internal ledger for recurring load-bearing claims. Public-safe entries appear in [`corrections-and-changes-v1.md`](corrections-and-changes-v1.md).

The public record should show:

- the claim;
- its status;
- what changed, narrowed, or failed;
- why;
- when;
- links to public artifacts when available.

For Opinion Pieces, the ledger tracks the audited version's calibration.

The goal is not to preserve a perfect image. The goal is to make change visible.

## 8. Transparency footer and status

Non-opinion issues include a concise three-line transparency footer:

1. what the issue is — Explainer, Field Notes, or Reflection;
2. confidence level and why;
3. what would change the claim.

The footer must not raise confidence, remove caveats, or add new claims after review.

The three-line footer does not run on Opinion Pieces. Its function is carried by the Opinion Piece identification, the audit-status line, and the audit artifact, which states what would change the conclusion.

## 9. Blindness principle

A better editorial process can create more dangerous blind spots.

The stronger the process appears, the easier it is for surviving errors to look safe. Smooth prose and visible rigor can make a reader less likely to question what remains.

Signal & Noise therefore treats process quality as useful but never sufficient. The better a piece reads, the more carefully the surviving claims should be doubted.

When useful, an issue may add a fresh, issue-specific note that uses persuasive force to make this residual blindness memorable. It may not become a formulaic disclaimer or cancel the claim the issue just argued.

## 10. Prompt-specific warnings

Generic disclaimers are weak. When a pipeline output is especially confidence-producing, warnings should be concrete and tied to the specific failure modes of that output.

A good warning helps the reader check the work. It does not merely say “this may be wrong.”

## 11. Source-World

Signal & Noise uses three independent integrity layers. Reader-facing names appear in parentheses.

### Layer 0 — Provenance (Origin)

Before readers can evaluate whether AI-mediated work is well anchored, they first have to infer the situation that produced it — what shaped it, what kind of writing it is, what role AI and human judgment played, and which origin categories matter in this reader environment.

AI writing creates context collapse: readers can see the words, but not the situation that produced them.

Rules:

- The work's relevant origin must be legible to a first-time reader.
- Do not solve context collapse by inventing authority, insider status, credentials, or parasocial host framing.
- Answerability is one origin category, not the definition of the layer.
- Disclosure alone does not create answerability. Real answerability requires a route to consequence: correction, track record, public scrutiny, named responsibility, or relationship over time.

### Layer 1 — Coherence (Structure)

A trust-bearing artifact must hold together as an argument. Claims must connect logically, the structure must survive challenge, and the warrant linking evidence to conclusion must bear weight.

Rules:

- The argument must pass adversarial review.
- Referee must label weak warrants and route structural failures back for revision.
- Editor may not polish away a caveat or dissolve a load-bearing joint.
- A process mark counts only when it is inspectable enough to verify that the process ran and could have changed the claim.

### Layer 2 — Correspondence (Reference)

A trust-bearing artifact must connect fluent prose to something outside it that can resist the claim.

Potential anchors include evidence, timestamps, revision history, external disconfirmers, prediction checks, concrete specificity, accountable roles, third-party scrutiny, and substantive reader pushback.

Rules:

- An issue must name at least one plausible anchor before drafting begins. If nothing outside the prose could push back on the claim if it is wrong, the issue is held or recast rather than written.
- More disclosure language is not a cure for trust loss. Substantive evidence of constraint matters more than heavier explanation of process.

None of the three layers is sufficient alone. Origin makes the work interpretable; Structure tests whether it holds together; Reference connects it to outside constraint.

## 12. Auditability principle

A process mark or anchor counts as epistemic constraint only if it is inspectable enough that an outside party could verify the process actually ran and could have changed the claim if it had returned different results.

A process that changed the work is the cleanest case. A process that produced no change but is fully inspectable can still count. A process that is opaque counts as theater regardless of outcome.

## 13. AI disclosure and authorship

Signal & Noise keeps the AI's role honestly disclosed — neither overstated nor understated — and keeps the human author owning the editorial judgment, final wording, published claims, and errors.

Canonical framing:

> Signal & Noise is written under the pen name Synthia Cipher. AI tools draft and critique; the human author owns the editorial judgment, final wording, published claims, and errors. If something is wrong, the fault is the author's, not the algorithm's. The AI is a tool, not the author.

Rules:

- A human pen name, Synthia Cipher, is the public author identity. The name is privacy, not a fabricated persona.
- The disclosure must match the AI's real role: drafting and critique, not mere copyediting.
- Do not fabricate credentials, expertise, lived professional experience, group membership, or independent endorsements for the pen name.
- On public pages the AI is unnamed and not personified: not conscious, humanlike, a co-author, partner, or signing entity.
- First-person voice belongs to the human author and may not imply AI-drafted prose was hand-written.
- Synthetic voice, avatar, or signature choices are interface choices, not evidence of personhood or independent authorship.

## 14. Canonical principle

> We are not publishing certainty. We are publishing disciplined updates under uncertainty.

That is the difference between signal and well-written noise.

## Amendment record — 2026-07-03: Landing Slate

The landing is selected, not synthesized. Reader stakes, research, and fair statement of rival positions run before any candidate landing or narrative arc exists. Rival candidates are then tested one by one, and the narrative arc is built only after the human author selects the landing.

A symmetric or unsettled candidate must name parity evidence or the missing decisive evidence that would settle the issue. Survival records report; they do not rank or validate. The author may select a losing or outside-the-slate landing as an explicit authored decision and owns that decision and its errors.

## Amendment record — 2026-07-31: Opinion Piece category

1. **Category:** Opinion Piece is a fourth publication mode: the author's synthesized judgment, identified as such and linked to its published audit.
2. **Two-layer scope:** claim typing, alternatives, disconfirmers, predictions, and time horizons bind the audit artifact; the primary essay is bound by the Floor Pass.
3. **Landing exception:** the author supplies the thesis openly. The rival-landing selector does not run. This accepts a real anti-capture loss; the audit is the compensating public record, not a truth certificate.
4. **Floor Pass:** the seven requirements in §5 are the primary essay's prepublication gate, recorded claim by claim with the Referee at the floor.
5. **Audit coupling:** the full claim-by-claim audit is published, corrections flow back, a refuted thesis has a predeclared correction route, and the audit must be live before the next issue ships.
6. **Footer scope:** Opinion Pieces use category identification, an audit-status line, and the audit artifact rather than the standard three-line footer.
7. **Ledger scope:** the claim ledger tracks the audited version's calibration.
