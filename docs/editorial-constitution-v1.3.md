## Changelog — v1.3 (2026-08-08)

**Summary:** v1.3 records the largest structural change in the publication's history: the **streamlined pipeline**, adopted 2026-08-08 after a review concluded the accumulated process had outgrown the time available to run it (its own record: ≈23 process obligations added against 7 retired by decision count — and almost none of the retirements had actually operated before the final pre-adoption package). Signal & Noise now publishes **one kind of piece — a two-layer piece**: the essay the author wanted to write, and a published audit of what the machine found when it checked. The former companion opinion publication (Intuition) retires as a separate masthead; its published issues remain archived unchanged. The audit stage becomes autonomous — AI-executed and machine-published under mandatory execution-mode disclosure — while the author retains every essay surface and every irreversible act.

**Changes:**

- Collapsed the four publication modes into one: the two-layer piece, governed by the Opinion Piece architecture (identification, Floor Pass, published audit, audit coupling). The three-line transparency footer is retired; its function is carried by category identification, the audit-status line, and the audit artifact.
- Retired the generative selection machinery (Landing Slate, rival-landing selector, protected-essentials workflow) for new pieces. The author develops the essay directly, outside the machine; the anti-capture loss this accepts is disclosed, and the published audit is the compensating record, not a truth certificate.
- Replaced the multi-gate pipeline with three stages: essay development (author + AI conversation, no process), a floor pass (AI-run accuracy-and-fairness redline; the author rules on every failure), and an autonomous published audit.
- Extended the Floor Pass: quotations must also be correctly attributed to their occasion; the calibration rule now covers all statistically qualified claims, not only quantitative ones; every piece must carry at least one outside-the-prose anchor; and the author's explicit right to override a floor failure with recorded ownership of the risk is restated.
- **Replaced the manual-execution rule for outside adversarial review.** The prior rule — external critique had to be run by hand by the author, never by the AI process through an API — was originally adopted after the AI process itself violated the then-policy by running two review gates through an automated fallback. It is now deleted in favor of what it was protecting: every audit page must state its execution mode — which models, which transport, what independence discount — and an undisclosed substitution remains a violation. The author holds a standing spot-check right over any audit run.
- Granted the audit stage the publication's first standing machine-publish authority: audit pages and the ledger and quarterly surfaces on the audit site. This is stated as the new authority it is. The author alone publishes, sends, schedules, and uploads everything readers subscribe to.
- The audit's published record is constrained to three lists — objections found, changes made or proposed, and what could not be checked — plus per-claim calibration. Never a survival verdict. A blocked check is reported as blocked, not as a pass.
- The audit-before-next-issue coupling is retained unchanged.

**Change types:** structural consolidation; new commitment; authority change (disclosed); scope changes; rule replacement with disclosed origin.

**Affected public artifacts:** this snapshot; the About page; the probe-match methods page (its per-issue instrument is retired — a dated supersession note marks it); future per-issue audit pages.

## 0. Purpose

Signal & Noise exists to reduce self-deception while making ideas usable.

**Core rule:** editorial usefulness may simplify expression, but may never override technical validity at the load-bearing layer.

The publication is not trying to present certainty. It is trying to publish disciplined updates under uncertainty.

## 1. The two-layer piece

Every Signal & Noise piece has two layers, both public:

- **The essay** — the author's synthesized judgment, presented as such: what the author wanted to say, the way a human wants to say it.
- **The audit** — a published record of what checking found: objections raised, what was verified against sources and logic, what changed, and what could not be checked.

The pair is the product. The gap between what the author wanted to be true and what checking established is displayed on every piece rather than smoothed away.

### Truth layer and narrative layer

Claims are typed before they carry weight — Observation, Interpretation, Hypothesis, Recommendation — and every framework-level hypothesis carries disconfirmers, a prediction, a time horizon, and at least two alternative hypotheses. **These duties bind the audit artifact.** The essay is bound by the Floor Pass (§3): language may be simplified and conviction may be voiced, but uncertainty removed for readability must remain inspectable in the audit.

## 2. The pipeline

### Stage A — essay development

The author develops the essay in direct conversation with a frontier AI model, outside any process. No gates run here. This is where intuition, conviction, and voice belong. The author may export the development conversation for a public origin record; if exported, it publishes only after a privacy and identification review and the author's explicit approval.

This openly accepts an anti-capture loss: no rival-thesis selection runs. The audit is the compensating public record, not proof the judgment is right.

### Stage B — the floor pass

Before publication, the AI process checks the essay against the floor (§3) and returns a redline. Floor failures block publication unless the author explicitly overrides with recorded ownership of the risk; otherwise the author chooses each remedy — support the claim or cut it — never whether to remedy. The author then publishes. Publishing, sending, scheduling, and uploading are always the author's acts.

### Stage C — the audit

The audit runs autonomously — by default against the published essay; before publication when the author designates a piece high-stakes — and publishes its record in The World Behind the Words. The timing trade is stated as a trade: judgment-class defects that the old pre-publication gates blocked can now ship first and return as corrections. Its binding constraints:

1. **Traces or it didn't happen.** Every check leaves an inspectable record of what was checked, how, and what could have changed the claim. "Ran, passed" is process theater and never appears.
2. **Kills, changes, and blind spots — never verdicts.** The audit page is three lists: objections found (with dispositions), changes made or proposed, and what could not be checked. A blocked check is reported as blocked — never as a pass, never as a zero. No survival counts, badges, or validation language. Per-claim calibration — claim type, evidentiary confidence, what would change the conclusion — is required reporting, not a verdict.
3. **Detection is separated from mutation.** Findings are produced with no edit authority, then confirmed or refuted in fresh context; only confirmed findings become proposed changes.
4. **Cross-model execution, disclosed.** Critique runs on independent frontier models via API. Every audit page states its execution mode: which models, which transport, what independence discount. An undisclosed substitution is a violation.
5. **The standing residual is disclosed.** The record shows some defect classes are caught by human cold reads, not machine checks. The audit names that blind class instead of implying coverage.
6. **No write authority.** The audit proposes and stages; it never edits a published essay. Floor-class findings route through the public corrections regime; a refuted thesis routes through a predeclared path — standfirst amendment, re-label, or retraction — the author's call, but the route exists in advance.
7. **The coupling holds:** the audit for issue N must be live before issue N+1 ships.

## 3. The floor

Every essay passes this floor before publication. No false or fraudulent claims, operationalized as:

1. **Accuracy:** figures exact; quotations verbatim and correctly attributed to their occasion, dated where load-bearing; verified against primary sources at pass time, with a paper's underlying specifications taking priority over its abstract or press coverage.
2. **Named-person fairness:** claims about a named person's views, motives, or conduct are anchored or clearly framed as the author's account of how the conduct lands.
3. **Receipt or cut:** unquantified-frequency claims and claims that something does not exist require support or removal.
4. **Calibration:** statistically qualified claims keep the qualifier in plain language or are restated below the evidence's ceiling.
5. **Identification:** the piece is identified as opinion where readers arrive — subject line, archive listing, on-page standfirst — plus a link to its audit status, without stacked in-text disclaimers.
6. **Integrity:** the essay is coherent, borrows no process claims it did not earn, and corrections extend rather than silently rewrite the public record.
7. **No empty hedging:** a hedge remains only when it carries real epistemic weight.
8. **Identity:** AI disclosure matches the AI's real role; no invented credentials, experience, or endorsements for the pen name or in copy; the AI is unnamed and not personified on public surfaces; AI-generated audio or visual assets are disclosed where material.
9. **An anchor exists:** at least one outside-the-prose anchor that could resist the claim if it is wrong. If nothing outside the prose could push back, the piece is held or recast rather than written.

## 4. Blindness principle

A better editorial process can create more dangerous blind spots. The stronger the process appears, the easier it is for surviving errors to look safe. Signal & Noise treats process quality as useful but never sufficient: the better a piece reads, the more carefully the surviving claims should be doubted. This applies with full force to the audit itself — an audit page is a record of what was checked, not a reason to stop checking.

## 5. Claim ledger and public corrections

Signal & Noise maintains an internal ledger for recurring load-bearing claims; the audit stage keeps it on calendar. Public-safe entries appear in [`corrections-and-changes-v1.md`](corrections-and-changes-v1.md): the claim, its status, what changed or failed, why, and when. A quarterly "what we changed our mind about" update is drafted and published by the audit stage under the same disclosed authority; current practice stages it for the author's review first — a practice, not a constitutional limit. The goal is not to preserve a perfect image. The goal is to make change visible.

## 6. Source-World

Signal & Noise uses three independent integrity layers. Reader-facing names in parentheses.

- **Layer 0 — Provenance (Origin):** the work's relevant origin must be legible to a first-time reader. Context collapse is not solved by inventing authority, insider status, or parasocial framing. Disclosure alone does not create answerability; answerability requires a route to consequence.
- **Layer 1 — Coherence (Structure):** the argument must hold together and survive challenge. A process mark counts only when it is inspectable enough to verify the process ran and could have changed the claim.
- **Layer 2 — Correspondence (Reference):** fluent prose must connect to something outside it that can resist the claim — evidence, disconfirmers, prediction checks, accountable roles, third-party scrutiny.

None of the three layers is sufficient alone.

## 7. Auditability principle

A process mark or anchor counts as epistemic constraint only if it is inspectable enough that an outside party could verify the process actually ran and could have changed the claim if it had returned different results. A process that changed the work is the cleanest case. A fully inspectable process that produced no change can still count. An opaque process counts as theater regardless of outcome.

## 8. AI disclosure and authorship

Canonical framing:

> Signal & Noise is written under the pen name Synthia Cipher. AI tools draft and critique; the human author owns the editorial judgment, final wording, published claims, and errors. If something is wrong, the fault is the author's, not the algorithm's. The AI is a tool, not the author.

Rules:

- The pen name is privacy, not a fabricated persona.
- The disclosure must match the AI's real role: drafting and critique, not mere copyediting. Under the streamlined pipeline this includes the audit: audit pages are AI-executed and machine-published, and each states its execution mode.
- No fabricated credentials, expertise, lived experience, group membership, or endorsements — for the pen name or anywhere in copy.
- On public pages the AI is unnamed and not personified: not conscious, humanlike, a co-author, partner, or signing entity.
- First-person voice belongs to the human author and may not imply AI-drafted prose was hand-written.
- The human author retains every essay surface and every irreversible act: publish, send, schedule, upload.

## 9. Canonical principle

> We are not publishing certainty. We are publishing disciplined updates under uncertainty.

That is the difference between signal and well-written noise.

## Amendment record — 2026-08-08: Streamlined pipeline

1. **One mode:** the two-layer piece (essay + published audit), under the former Opinion Piece architecture extended to every piece. The four-mode system and the three-line footer are retired.
2. **Three stages:** author-led essay development with no process; an AI-run floor pass with the author ruling on every failure (explicit override with recorded ownership remains available); an autonomous published audit.
3. **Machinery retired:** Landing Slate, rival-landing selector, per-issue protected-essentials workflow, and the standalone external-review gates. Their critique duties re-scope to the audit stage.
4. **Manual-execution rule replaced, origin disclosed:** the old rule requiring hand-run external critique was adopted after the AI process violated the then-policy via an automated fallback; its successor is a mandatory execution-mode line on every audit page plus a standing author spot-check right.
5. **New authority, named:** the audit stage machine-publishes audit pages and the ledger and quarterly surfaces on the audit site — the publication's first standing AI publish authority. Essay surfaces and all irreversible distribution acts remain the author's alone.
6. **Companion masthead retired:** the sibling opinion publication's issues remain archived unchanged; the gap it displayed across two publications is now displayed inside every piece.
7. **Unchanged:** the audit coupling, the corrections regime, the Blindness and Auditability principles, and the AI-disclosure rules.
